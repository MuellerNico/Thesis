\section{Usage instructions}

This appendix is a practical guide for running the MHD part of SPH-EXA. It assumes
familiarity with the hydrodynamics side of the code: building, launching, glass blocks,
HDF5 dumps and restarts all work exactly as before. Only what is specific to MHD is
described here. The code is available in the
\href{https://github.com/MuellerNico/sphexa/commits/develop/}{\textbf{GitHub fork}}.

The MHD extension adds one propagator (\texttt{magneto-ve}), a set of magnetized test
cases, a handful of command-line flags, and a group of particle fields that are written
to the HDF5 dumps under a \texttt{magneto::} prefix. Nothing else in the workflow changes.

\subsection{Running a simulation}

The same executable is used as for pure hydrodynamics
(\texttt{sphexa} on CPU, \texttt{sphexa-cuda} on GPU). A minimal run:

\begin{verbatim}
sphexa --init orszag-tang --glass 50c.h5 --prop magneto-ve \
       -n 200 -s 1.0 -w 0.1 -o dump.h5
\end{verbatim}

Two things must be consistent:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The propagator must be an MHD propagator.} Use \texttt{--prop magneto-ve}
          for all test cases, or \texttt{--prop magneto-turb-ve} for driven turbulence.
          With any other propagator the magnetic fields are never allocated and the run is
          plain hydrodynamics.
    \item \textbf{The initial conditions must set a magnetic field.} Either one of the
          magnetized test cases below, or a restart file / initial-condition file that
          contains the magnetic fields. Combining \texttt{--prop magneto-ve} with a purely
          hydrodynamic initializer leaves $\mathbf{B}$ uninitialized.
\end{enumerate}

The code works in units with $\mu_0 = 1$, so the magnetic pressure is $B^2/2$ and the
Alfv\'en speed is $v_A = |\mathbf{B}|/\sqrt{\rho}$. All test-case constants are given in
these units.

\subsection{Test cases}

The magnetized test cases are selected with \texttt{--init}. Most of them require a glass
block (\texttt{--glass}); the two shock-type tests can alternatively be built on a regular
lattice by omitting \texttt{--glass}. All boxes are fully periodic. The quasi-2D tests use
a thin slab in $z$ whose thickness is set automatically from the resolution.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{7.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\texttt{--init} & glass & description \\
\midrule
\texttt{alfven-wave}              & required & Circularly polarized Alfv\'en wave travelling obliquely through the box. Standard convergence test. Box $[0,3] \times [0,1.5]^2$. \\
\texttt{brio-wu}                  & optional & MHD shock tube, thin box $[-2,2]$ in $x$. Without \texttt{--glass} a regular lattice is used. \\
\texttt{mhd-loop}                 & optional & Advection of a weak magnetic field loop through a $2{:}1$ box; measures numerical dissipation. Without \texttt{--glass} an FCC (or simple-cubic) lattice is used. \\
\texttt{orszag-tang}              & required & Orszag--Tang vortex, unit box, thin in $z$. Standard 2D MHD benchmark. \\
\texttt{mhd-rotor}                & required & Rotating dense disk in a uniform field, box $[-0.5,0.5]^2$, thin in $z$. \\
\texttt{sedov-magneto}            & required & Sedov blast wave with a uniform background field along $(\hat{x}+\hat{z})/\sqrt{2}$. \\
\texttt{kelvin-helmholtz-magneto} & required & Kelvin--Helmholtz instability with a uniform field along $x$. \\
\texttt{turbulence-magneto}       & required & Driven supersonic turbulence with a uniform mean field along $z$. Requires \texttt{--prop magneto-turb-ve}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Magnetized test cases.}
\end{table}

The resolution flag \texttt{-n} keeps its usual meaning of the effective number of
particles per box length for all glass-based cases and for the \texttt{mhd-loop} lattice
variant. For the \texttt{brio-wu} lattice variant it is instead the number of lattice
planes along $x$ in the low-density half of the tube, so that the setup of
Wissing \& Shen (2020) is reproduced exactly.

\subsection{Command-line options}

\texttt{sphexa --help} lists all flags. The MHD-relevant ones are:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{7.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
flag & default & meaning \\
\midrule
\texttt{--prop} & \texttt{ve} & \texttt{magneto-ve} for MHD, \texttt{magneto-turb-ve} for driven MHD turbulence. \\
\texttt{--resistivity} & \texttt{SLR} & Artificial resistivity scheme, see below. \\
\texttt{--arfloor NUM} & \texttt{1.0} & Floor $F$ in the modulated resistivity clamp $L_{ij} = \max(F, \text{modulator})$. Only used by \texttt{SLRB}/\texttt{SLRB2}; $F=1$ disables the modulation. \\
\texttt{--conductivity NUM} & \texttt{0} & Artificial conductivity coefficient $\alpha_u$. \texttt{0} disables the heat-conduction term. \\
\texttt{--no-slr} & off & Disables slope-limited reconstruction in the \emph{artificial viscosity}. Independent of \texttt{--resistivity}. \\
\texttt{--avswitches} & off & Enables the artificial-viscosity switches. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{MHD-relevant command-line flags.}
\end{table}

The \texttt{--resistivity} flag accepts:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{switch} --- the Tricco \& Price (2013) switch.
    \item \texttt{SLR} (default) --- slope-limited reconstruction of $\mathbf{B}$ across the
          particle pair.
    \item \texttt{SLRB} / \texttt{SLRB2} --- as \texttt{SLR}, with an additional Balsara-like
          modulation of the dissipation amplitude (power 1 and 2 respectively), tunable with
          \texttt{--arfloor}.
    \item a number, e.g. \texttt{--resistivity 0.5} --- constant $\alpha_B$.
\end{itemize}

Note the naming collision: \texttt{--no-slr} switches off slope-limited reconstruction in
the \emph{viscous} dissipation of the momentum and energy equations, whereas
\texttt{--resistivity SLR} selects the reconstruction-based scheme for the \emph{magnetic}
dissipation. The two are set independently.

\subsection{Changing test-case parameters}

Every test case defines its physical constants (densities, pressures, field strengths,
$\gamma$, $K_\mathrm{cour}$, $n_{g0}$, \dots) as named defaults. They are overridden by
passing an HDF5 settings file after a colon:

\begin{verbatim}
sphexa --init mhd-loop:settings.h5 --prop magneto-ve ...
\end{verbatim}

The file contains the overridden values as file attributes. It can be created with
\texttt{scripts/set\_parms.py}, or inline in a job script:

\begin{verbatim}
python - settings.h5 <<'EOF'
import sys, h5py
with h5py.File(sys.argv[1], "w") as f:
    f.attrs["rhoIn"] = 2.0
    f.attrs["Kcour"] = 0.1
EOF
\end{verbatim}

At startup the code prints each attribute it read, and warns about names it does not
recognize --- a useful check against typos. The most relevant magnetic constants are:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
test case & attribute & default \\
\midrule
\texttt{alfven-wave}              & \texttt{noiseAmp} (white-noise amplitude on $\mathbf{B}$) & \texttt{0} \\
\texttt{brio-wu}                  & \texttt{Bx}, \texttt{ByL}, \texttt{ByR} & \texttt{0.75}, \texttt{1}, \texttt{-1} \\
\texttt{mhd-loop}                 & \texttt{A0} (loop amplitude), \texttt{R0} (radius) & \texttt{1e-3}, \texttt{0.3} \\
\texttt{mhd-loop}                 & \texttt{rhoIn}/\texttt{rhoOut} (density-jump variant) & \texttt{1}/\texttt{1} \\
\texttt{mhd-loop}                 & \texttt{fccLattice} (1 = FCC, 0 = simple cubic) & \texttt{1} \\
\texttt{orszag-tang}              & \texttt{B0} & $1/\sqrt{4\pi}$ \\
\texttt{mhd-rotor}                & \texttt{Bx} & $5/\sqrt{4\pi}$ \\
\texttt{sedov-magneto}            & \texttt{Bmag} & \texttt{10} \\
\texttt{turbulence-magneto}       & \texttt{Bmag} & \texttt{0.15} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Magnetic parameters of the test cases, overridable through a settings file.}
\end{table}

Setting the field strength to zero is a convenient way to obtain a directly comparable
hydrodynamic reference run of the same setup.

\subsection{Output and restart}

The magnetic fields are written into the same HDF5 dumps as the hydrodynamic ones, under
the prefix \texttt{magneto::} (e.g.\ \texttt{magneto::Bx}). On the command line the fields
are named \emph{without} the prefix:

\begin{verbatim}
-f x,y,z,h,m,rho,vx,vy,vz,Bx,By,Bz,divB
\end{verbatim}

If \texttt{-f} is omitted, all conserved fields are written and the dump is restartable.
The conserved magnetic fields are \texttt{Bx}, \texttt{By}, \texttt{Bz}, their time
derivatives \texttt{dBx}, \texttt{dBy}, \texttt{dBz} and previous-step values
\texttt{dBx\_m1}, \texttt{dBy\_m1}, \texttt{dBz\_m1}, plus the divergence-cleaning
variables \texttt{psi\_ch}, \texttt{d\_psi\_ch}, \texttt{d\_psi\_ch\_m1}. \textbf{If you
pass a custom \texttt{-f} list and want to restart from the dump, all of these must be
included.} Restarting works as in the hydrodynamic case:

\begin{verbatim}
sphexa --init dump.h5:3 --prop magneto-ve -s 500 -o restart.h5
\end{verbatim}

Useful non-conserved (diagnostic) fields are \texttt{divB} (divergence of $\mathbf{B}$),
\texttt{curlB\_x}, \texttt{curlB\_y}, \texttt{curlB\_z} (current density up to $\mu_0$),
\texttt{gradB\_norm} (norm of $\nabla \mathbf{B}$) and \texttt{alpha\_B} (the local
artificial-resistivity coefficient). They cost memory and I/O bandwidth, so only request
them when needed.

\subsection{Scalar diagnostics: \texttt{constants.txt}}

As in hydrodynamics, one line per iteration is appended to \texttt{constants.txt} next to
the output file. MHD runs add four columns. In order:

\begin{verbatim}
iteration  ttot  minDt  etot  ecin  eint  egrav  linmom  angmom
           eMag  meanDivBError  maxDivBError  resHeating  [test-specific]
\end{verbatim}

\texttt{eMag} is the total magnetic energy and is included in \texttt{etot}, so energy
conservation can be monitored directly. The two divergence columns report the
dimensionless error $h\,|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}|/|\mathbf{B}|$, averaged over all particles
and as a global maximum. \texttt{resHeating} is the instantaneous total heating rate
produced by the artificial resistivity. The trailing column is present only for the test
cases that define one (RMS Mach number for turbulence, mode amplitude for
Kelvin--Helmholtz).

\subsection{Plotting}

Two scripts in \texttt{scripts/} cover most needs. Both are plain Python
(\texttt{numpy}, \texttt{h5py}, \texttt{matplotlib}) and are usually called at the end of
a job script.

\subsubsection*{\texttt{plot\_constants.py} --- diagnostics dashboard}

\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/plot_constants.py out/123456/constants.txt
\end{verbatim}

Produces \texttt{constants\_diagnostics.pdf} next to the input file: energy components,
relative total-energy drift, linear and angular momentum, magnetic energy normalized to
its initial value, the mean and maximum $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}$ error, the time step, and
the resistive heating rate together with its cumulative integral. The magnetic-energy
panel also shows the dissipation-only expectation
$1 - \int \dot{E}_\mathrm{res}\,\mathrm{d}t / E_\mathrm{mag,0}$; the gap between the two
curves is magnetic energy created by the ideal induction term. Use
\texttt{--kind kh} to label the trailing column of a Kelvin--Helmholtz run correctly
(default \texttt{turb}).

\subsubsection*{\texttt{plot\_slice.py} --- 2D slices}

Renders an SPH-interpolated slice of any field through the box:

\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/plot_slice.py dump.h5 -i                 # metadata + field list
python scripts/plot_slice.py dump.h5 --field rho        # final step
python scripts/plot_slice.py dump.h5 5 --field Bmag -l  # step 5, log scale
python scripts/plot_slice.py dump.h5 --all --field Bmag -j 8
\end{verbatim}

A file may be followed by a step number (negative counts from the end, absent means the
last step). Several files are tiled into a single figure sharing one color scale, which is
the standard way to compare resolutions or dissipation schemes:

\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/plot_slice.py a.h5 b.h5 c.h5 --labels 128 256 512 --clean
\end{verbatim}

The most useful options are \texttt{--field}, \texttt{--axis}/\texttt{--pos} (slice plane;
by default the box midplane normal to $z$), \texttt{-r} (grid resolution),
\texttt{--cmap}, \texttt{-l} (log scale), \texttt{--vmin}/\texttt{--vmax},
\texttt{--fieldlines} (streamplot overlay, using $\mathbf{B}$ by default),
\texttt{--smooth} (widen the rendering kernel for a smoother picture),
\texttt{--scatter} (skip the interpolation, fast preview), \texttt{-j} (parallel workers,
pass \texttt{"\$SLURM\_CPUS\_PER\_TASK"} in a job script) and \texttt{--clean} (PDF output
without title for use as a figure). \texttt{-h} lists the rest.

Besides the raw datasets, the script understands derived fields computed on the fly:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
name & quantity \\
\midrule
\texttt{Bmag}, \texttt{vmag}       & $|\mathbf{B}|$, $|\mathbf{v}|$ \\
\texttt{Emag}, \texttt{Pmag}       & magnetic energy density, magnetic pressure \\
\texttt{KE}                        & kinetic energy density \\
\texttt{curlBmag}                  & $|\nabla \times \mathbf{B}|$ \\
\texttt{divBerr}, \texttt{log\_divBerr} & $h\,|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}|/|\mathbf{B}|$ (and its $\log_{10}$) \\
\texttt{Bphi}, \texttt{Bperp}, \texttt{Bperp\_rel} & loop-frame decomposition for the \texttt{mhd-loop} test \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Derived fields available to \texttt{plot\_slice.py}. \texttt{-i} lists those
resolvable from a given dump.}
\end{table}

Both scripts only need the dump and are independent of the simulation build, so they can
be run on a login node or locally.

\subsection{Caveats}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}$ diagnostic is unreliable where the field is
          weak.} The reported quantity is normalized by $|\mathbf{B}|$, so in regions with
          $\mathbf{B} \approx 0$ it is dominated by noise and can be arbitrarily large.
          Particles with exactly $\mathbf{B} = 0$ are excluded, but near-zero ones are not.
          This affects tests with field-free regions, in particular \texttt{mhd-loop}
          (everything outside the loop) and any field null. Compare the \emph{mean} column
          across runs of the same setup rather than reading the maximum as an absolute
          quality measure.
    \item \textbf{Divergence-cleaning parameters are fixed} at $f_\mathrm{clean} =
          \sigma_c = 1$ and are not exposed on the command line. A more aggressive
          cleaning speed would require an additional Courant limit on the time step.
    \item \textbf{No block time stepping.} There is no MHD analogue of the \texttt{ve-bdt}
          propagator; all particles are advanced on a global time step.
    \item \textbf{The time step does not include a limit from the artificial resistivity.}
          With large $\alpha_B$ (e.g.\ \texttt{--resistivity 1}) at high resolution, reduce
          \texttt{Kcour} if the magnetic energy behaves erratically.
    \item \textbf{Self-gravitating MHD is untested.} Nothing prevents combining
          \texttt{--prop magneto-ve} with self-gravity, but neither the time-step control
          nor the Lorentz force have been validated in that regime.
    \item \textbf{Artificial conductivity assumes an ideal-gas equation of state.} Do not
          combine \texttt{--conductivity} with the isothermal or polytropic EOS.
    \item \textbf{Exact energy conservation of the cleaning terms assumes equal particle
          masses}, which all built-in test cases satisfy.
\end{itemize}

\section{Details of code implementation}
Software documentation for the SPH-EXA team and future developers of the code. 

Need full velocity Jacobian $J_v$ for induction. Thus, overridden IAD driver. 

branching on curlv allocated? new neighbor lists. gradh correction.

hardcoded divB cleaning variables. $f_{clean}=\sigma_c = 1$. Would need courant limit if raising fclean. evolve psi/ch. Only conservative conjugate pair for equal particle masses. mj/mi resiudal factor. 

divBerr diagnostic unreliable on domains with B=0 regions/spots. 

Using SLR: \texttt{T du\_diss = diss\_op * dot(B\_ab\_raw, B\_ab);} instead of \texttt{norm2(B\_ab)}.
energy conjugate of dBdiss: dE\_B/dt goes as B\_ab . B'\_ab, not |B'\_ab|2. Identical for the non-reconstructing schemes (B'\_ab == B\_ab). The transverse part of the SLR correction does no work on the field, so charging heat for it would leak energy.

No block time step. self-gravitating MHD untested (timestep control, limit lorentz force movement)
