\section{Theory}
\subsection{SPH Theory}
Euler equations. Particles, neighbors and kernels. Volume elements and gradient evaluation. gradh (chainrule origin, variable smoothing lengths). Conservation vs accuracy. TSPH vs GDSPH vs ISPH (IAD/GVE) discretization. GDSPH: Density discontinuities at contact surfaces (e.g. 2 different mediums). ISPH: Perfect conservation but not perfect linear interpolation. Partition of unity (makes interpolation nearly exact), strong when disordered particles or near discontinuities. Accounts for uneven sampling in space (similar to weighted least squares).

\subsection{Artificial Resistivity} 
Discontinuities, stability, motivation. 

Finite particles cannot represent a true discontinuity which would be true physical solution for a shock or sharp gradient. So energy in the sharp gradient has nowhere to go, can't propagate away cleanly. Instead bounces back as high-freq oscillations -> Gibbs-like ringing artifacts near shocks. Without dissipation those oscillations don't decay but grow and blow up solution. Just need to give energy somewhere to go, like a drain or lowpass filter at resolution scale. Rather dump energy locally than corrupt rest of solution. 

NOTE: Velocity noise results in magnetic dissipation via the signal velocity

\subsubsection{SLR}
Subtract linear component to remove dissipation in smooth flow. But reconstruction dangerous at shocks (overshoots/monotonicity violations, see TVD limiters). Need limiter to compare jacobians and only apply reconstruction if both sides "aggree", i.e. have very similar jacobians. 

Van Leer-style limiters not specific to velocity, general safety of linear reconstruction at any discontinuity. limiter kinda like a switch? 
Might need to check if the J\_B ratio actually is good at catching magnetic shocks. 

If particles get too close together ($q_{ab} < q_\text{crit}$) we force the limiter to zero with $\kappa$ to help us dissipate noise. Possibly only relevant for hydro, might get rid of kappa for resistivity. Except if tests show that kappa is a beneficial shock/noise indicator. 
\begin{equation}
B'_{a} \equiv B_{a} - \frac{1}{2} \phi_{ab} \mathbf{J}_{B_a} \mathbf{x}^T_{ab}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
B'_{b} \equiv B_{b} + \frac{1}{2} \phi_{ba} \mathbf{J}_{B_b} \mathbf{x}^T_{ab}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\phi_{ab} = \max\left[0, \min\left(1, \frac{4F_{ab}}{(1 + F_{ab})^2}\right)\right] \times \kappa_{ab}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\kappa_{ab} = \begin{cases} \exp\left\{-\left(\dfrac{q_{ab} - q_\text{crit}}{q_\text{fold}}\right)^2\right\} & \text{if } q_{ab} < q_\text{crit} \\ 1 & \text{if } q_{ab} \geq q_\text{crit} \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
q_{ab} \equiv \min(q_a, q_b)
\end{equation}

Mean interparticle spacing $q_\text{crit}$. Probably irrelevant for MHD, since resistivity doesn't really address the problem of particle clustering the same way viscosity does. 
\begin{equation}
q_\text{crit} \equiv \left(\frac{32\pi}{3 n b_a}\right)^{1/3}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
F_{ab} \equiv \frac{\mathbf{x}_{ab} \mathbf{J}_{B_a} \mathbf{x}^T_{ab}}{\mathbf{x}_{ab} \mathbf{J}_{B_b} \mathbf{x}^T_{ab}}
\end{equation}
